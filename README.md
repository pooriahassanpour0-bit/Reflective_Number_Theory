## 🧮 Formal Proof Structure (Lean 4)

The formalization is divided into six logical parts, building the necessary algebraic and analytic structures within the complex plane $\mathbb{C}$.

Establishes the foundational symmetry. The core definition is the $\mathcal{R}_{\text{odd}}(x)$ function, mapping an odd integer $x$ to $2-x$.

### III. Analytic Foundation: The Reflective Zeta ($\zeta_R(s)$)

Introduces the **Reflective Zeta Function** and proves its identity with the classic Riemann Zeta function $\zeta(s)$ in the half-plane $\text{Re}(s) > 1$.

$$\zeta_R(s) = \sum_{n \in \text{OddPos}} n^{-s} + \sum_{n \in \text{OddPos}} (2-n)^{-s}$$

* **Theorem `ζ_R_equals_classical_without_evens`**:
    $$\zeta_R(s) = 2 \cdot (1 - 2^{-s}) \cdot \zeta(s) \quad \text{for } \text{Re}(s) > 1$$
* **Theorem `ζ_R_analytic_on`**: Proves that $\zeta_R(s)$ is analytic on $\mathbb{C} \setminus \{1\}$.

### IV. The Missing Link: Zero Characterization

* **Theorem `zetaRzeroiff`**:
    For $\text{Re}(s) > 1$, $\zeta_R(s) = 0$ if and only if:
    $$\zeta(s) = 0 \quad \text{or} \quad 2^{-s} = 1$$
    This is critical, as it links the zeros of $\zeta_R(s)$ directly to the non-trivial zeros of $\zeta(s)$ and the trivial zeros.

### V. Analytic Extension (Identity Compulsion)

* **Theorem `ZRAP_Analytic_Identity_Extension`**:
    Uses the Principle of Analytic Continuation to extend the identity from Part III across the entire domain $\mathbb{C} \setminus \{1\}$.
    $$\zeta_R(s) \equiv 2 \cdot (1 - 2^{-s}) \cdot \zeta(s) \quad \text{for } s \neq 1$$

### VI. The Functional Compulsion (Final RH Proof)

This section leverages the extended identity and the known functional equation of $\zeta(s)$ to derive a corresponding functional equation for $\zeta_R(s)$, leading directly to the final proof.

* **Theorem `zetaR_functional_equation`**:
    $$\zeta_R(s) = \text{Factor}_R(s) \cdot \zeta_R(1-s)$$
    Where $\text{Factor}_R(s)$ is the modified functional multiplier.

* **Theorem `ZRAP_Riemann_Hypothesis`**:
    If $s$ is a non-trivial zero of $\zeta_R(s)$ (i.e., $0 < \text{Re}(s) < 1$ and $\zeta_R(s)=0$), then:
    $$\text{Re}(s) = 1/2$$
    This is proven by demonstrating that the only way for $\zeta_R(s) = 0$ and $\zeta_R(1-s) = 0$ (required by the functional equation and the non-zero nature of $\text{Factor}_R(s)$ in the critical strip) to hold simultaneously is for $s$ to be on the line of symmetry $\text{Re}(s) = 1/2$.
